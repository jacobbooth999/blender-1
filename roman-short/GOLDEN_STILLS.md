# From Wolf to Golden: 7 stills

Cartoon "2D characters in a 3D world" stills of how the golden retriever came to be.
Code: `src/videos/golden/` (composition `GoldenStills`, one frame per still).
Render: `node scripts/stills.mjs GoldenStills out/golden/final/g_ 0,1,2,3,4,5,6 1.3333` (1440x2560).

| # | Still | Era | What it shows | Fact behind it |
|---|-------|-----|---------------|----------------|
| 1 | `g_0` | Ice Age | A grey wolf howls from a snowy rock at sunset, with a second wolf below | All dogs come from an ancient wolf population. Dogs and wolves split roughly 15,000-40,000 years ago. |
| 2 | `g_1` | Stone Age, ~15,000 years ago | A hunter-gatherer kid sits by the fire with a half-tame dog, and a hunter with a spear stands behind | Dogs were the first domesticated animal. The Bonn-Oberkassel dog was buried with people about 14,000 years ago. |
| 3 | `g_2` | 1700s-1800s, Newfoundland | A black St. John's water dog brings a fish to a fisherman on the dock | The St. John's water dog, now extinct, is the ancestor of the modern retriever breeds. Fishing boats brought these dogs to Britain. |
| 4 | `g_3` | 1800s, River Tweed (Scottish border) | A curly, liver-coloured Tweed Water Spaniel on the riverbank with a ghillie | The Tweed Water Spaniel, also extinct, was a curly water dog from the Tweed valley. |
| 5 | `g_4` | 1868, Guisachan estate, Scotland | Sir Dudley Marjoribanks with Nous (yellow, wavy coat), Belle (Tweed Water Spaniel) and four yellow puppies | Nous and Belle had the first litter of four yellow puppies (Crocus, Cowslip, Primrose and Ada). That litter started the breed. |
| 6 | `g_5` | Early 1900s | A gamekeeper on a marsh at sunset, and his golden wading out with a duck | Goldens were bred as gundogs with a "soft mouth" for bringing back waterfowl. The Kennel Club recognised them in 1911 and named them "Golden Retriever" in 1920. |
| 7 | `g_6` | Today | A golden bounds across a backyard with a tennis ball while a kid cheers | The working gundog became one of the world's favourite family dogs. |

No text is baked into the images, so titles and captions can be added in the editor or the thumbnail tool.
