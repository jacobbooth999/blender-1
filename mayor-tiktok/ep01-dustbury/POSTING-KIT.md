## Why it's built this way (what the research says)
| Technique | Where it is in the video |
|---|---|
| Hook inside 1–2 s: motion, sound, and a bold claim | The sign slams down with a riser and a thud; the first line is "worst town on the internet" |
| Tell viewers it's a series from frame 1 | "EP. 1 · DAY 1" badge is always on screen |
| Visible stakes and progress meters | POP 3 counter, TOWN HEALTH 8%, coins 100 |
| Characters to care about | Old Rose, Gary, Dot, each with a name tag and a single joke |
| Re-hooks at about 15 s and 30 s | "But Dustbury still has people" (~19 s); record scratch, then the mystery building (~30 s) |
| Open loop / long-term mystery | The building on the hill: "We'll get to that." The window lights up |
| Ticking clock / loss | Rose is leaving; POP flashes 3→2?; the whole town sinks at "gone forever", then rewinds |
| Clear, low-effort choice + reason to comment | Two cards, A vs B, each with a preview of what it would look like |
| Avoid one-word "engagement bait" | It asks for **A or B + why**, with a shout-out reward. Single-token replies get discounted |
| Follow CTA tied to the story | "Follow to see what you built… or what you broke" |
| Loop | Ends on the same shot it opens on, so a replay flows straight back into the hook |
| Sound | Riser into the slam, whooshes on cuts, pops on big words, one deliberate silence before the mystery, and no wall-to-wall effects |
| Muted viewers | Kinetic captions carry every line |

The specific numbers in hook and retention studies come mostly from marketing blogs, so treat them as rough. Check **your own** retention graph: a drop at 0–3 s means the hook needs work; a drop around 8–10 s means the payoff came too slowly.
